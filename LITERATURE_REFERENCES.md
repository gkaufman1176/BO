## CATEGORY 1: COMPREHENSIVE REVIEWS & INDUSTRY PRIMERS

### 1. Plant Protein Resources, Novel Extraction and Precipitation Methods (2024)
**Citation:** Akyüz et al. (2024). Journal of Food Process Engineering.
**DOI:** 10.1111/jfpe.14758
**Key Findings:**
- Reviews plant protein resources including legumes, cereals, oilseeds
- Covers enzyme-assisted extraction, aqueous extraction, emerging technologies
- Novel precipitation methods: IEP, salting-out, membrane filtration
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Comprehensive overview of aqueous methods

### 2. Protein Extraction and Isolation from Legumes and Algae: An Industry Primer (2025)
**Citation:** Food and Bioprocess Technology (2025)
**DOI:** 10.1007/s11947-025-03958-8
**Key Findings:**
- Systematic comparison of extraction techniques for legumes
- Evaluation of efficiency, yield, purity, economic feasibility
- Alkaline-IEP is resource-intensive; enzymatic/UF preserve functionality but scalability limits
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Industry-focused, recent, economic analysis

### 3. Recent Progress in Plant-Based Proteins: Extraction and Modification Methods (2024)
**Citation:** PMC 11239452
**Key Findings:**
- Salt extraction: NaCl 0.2-0.8 M (salting-in vs salting-out)
- Enzymatic hydrolysis, ultrafiltration, fermentation methods
- Applications in food industry
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Covers all major methods

### 4. Opportunities and Challenges of Plant Proteins as Functional Ingredients (2024)
**Citation:** PNAS 2319019121
**Key Findings:**
- Functional properties required for food applications
- Challenges: solubility, emulsification, gelation
- Comparison across pea, soy, faba, lentil
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - More on functionality than extraction

## CATEGORY 2: SALT EXTRACTION + MEMBRANE FILTRATION

### 5. Salt Extraction + Ultrafiltration for Chickpea vs. Pea Protein
**Citation:** MDPI Foods 12(8):1694 (2023)
**Title:** Salt Solubilization Coupled with Membrane Filtration - Impact on Structure/Function of Chickpea Compared to Pea Protein
**Key Findings:**
- SE-UF validated for both chickpea and pea proteins
- Protein content: 86% (chickpea), 88-92% (pea)
- Yield: 82% (chickpea), 87% (pea)
- Similar structure/function between the two proteins
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - Multi-protein SE-UF validation

### 6. Hemp Protein: Alkaline-IEP vs. Salt Extraction
**Citation:** Food Chemistry (2022), PubMed 36435111
**Title:** Structural, functional properties, and volatile profile of hemp protein isolate as affected by extraction method: Alkaline extraction–isoelectric precipitation vs salt extraction
**Key Findings:**
- SE-UF: 88-97% protein content, 82-87% yield
- AE-IEP: 86-93% protein content, similar yield
- SE-UF produced HIGHER solubility (critical for beverages)
- Both methods achieved high purity
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - Hemp SE-UF validation, functional comparison

### 7. Ultrafiltration of Rapeseed Protein with Pectinase Pre-Treatment
**Citation:** MDPI Foods 13(15):2423 (2024)
**Title:** Ultrafiltration of Rapeseed Protein Concentrate: Effect of Pectinase Treatment on Membrane Fouling
**Key Findings:**
- Pectinase addition during extraction increased permeate flux
- Reduced irreversible fouling on UF membranes
- Applicable to high-pectin protein sources
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - Enzymatic + UF combination, anti-fouling strategy

### 8. Recovery of Protein from Enzyme-Assisted Aqueous Extraction by UF
**Citation:** PMC 6392832
**Title:** Recovery of high value-added protein from enzyme-assisted aqueous extraction (EAE) of soybeans by dead-end ultrafiltration
**Key Findings:**
- 5 kDa UF membrane: 80-91% protein rejection
- Protein purity in retentate: 73-82%
- EAE + UF combination effective for soy
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Enzymatic + UF validation for soy

## CATEGORY 3: ENZYMATIC PRE-TREATMENT

### 9. Hemp Protein: Enzymatic (Cellulase + Phytase) + UF
**Citation:** ScienceDirect (2016), doi S1466856415001599
**Title:** Conversion of a low protein hemp seed meal into a functional protein concentrate through enzymatic digestion of fibre coupled with membrane ultrafiltration
**Key Findings:**
- 37% protein hemp meal → 74% protein concentrate
- Carbohydrases + phytase treatment
- Protein digestibility: 89% (vs. <70% without enzymes)
- UF removed digested fiber products
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - Enzymatic + UF for hemp, high-fiber source case study

### 10. Enzyme-Assisted Extraction of Plant Proteins (General Review)
**Citation:** PMC 8876524 (2022)
**Title:** Enzymes-Assisted Extraction of Plants for Sustainable and Functional Applications
**Key Findings:**
- Cellulase, pectinase, hemicellulase break down cell walls
- Phytase reduces phytic acid (antinutrient)
- Mild conditions, substrate specificity, industrial applicability
- **Relevance:** HIGH - General enzymatic extraction principles

### 11. Enzyme-Aided Cell Wall Disintegration for Rapeseed Protein
**Citation:** JAFC (2014)
**Title:** Effect of Enzyme-Aided Cell Wall Disintegration on Protein Extractability from Intact and Dehulled Rapeseed Press Cakes
**Key Findings:**
- Enzyme treatment increased protein yield 15-20%
- Cellulase most effective for cell wall breakdown
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - Validates cellulase for oilseeds

## CATEGORY 4: REVERSIBLE SALT PRECIPITATION

### 12. Protein Precipitation Using Ammonium Sulfate (Protocol)
**Citation:** PMC 4817497 (2016), Current Protocols in Protein Science
**Title:** Protein Precipitation Using Ammonium Sulfate
**Key Findings:**
- Standard method for large-scale protein purification
- Salt precipitation does NOT denature proteins (reversible)
- Salting-out concentrations: 1.5-2.5 M (NH₄)₂SO₄
- Proteins can be resolubilized in low-salt buffer
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Standard protocol, reversibility confirmation

### 13. Hofmeister Series for Salting-Out
**Citation:** Org. Process Res. Dev. (2017)
**Title:** General Principles and Strategies for Salting-Out Informed by the Hofmeister Series
**Key Findings:**
- Anion series: citrate > sulfate > phosphate > chloride
- Cation series: ammonium > potassium > sodium
- (NH₄)₂SO₄ is most effective combination for salting-out
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - Theoretical basis for salt selection

## CATEGORY 6: ISOELECTRIC PRECIPITATION (STANDARD & MODIFIED)

### 16. Systematic Study of IEP on Soy Protein Isolate
**Citation:** ScienceDirect (2023), doi S0963996922012352
**Title:** A systematic study of the impact of the isoelectric precipitation process on the physical properties and protein composition of soy protein isolates
**Key Findings:**
- Standard IEP: pH 10-11 → pH 4.5 (pI of soy)
- Protein yield: 82%, purity: 90%+
- **Limitation:** Poor solubility at neutral pH (20-35%)
- **Relevance:** HIGH - IEP baseline for comparison

### 17. Functional Properties of Pea Protein from Different Cultivars

**Title:** Functional properties of protein isolates from different pea cultivars
**Key Findings:**
- Protein levels: 81-89% (IEP method)
- Isoelectric points: pH 4.7-4.9
- Cultivar variability in functionality
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - Pea IEP baseline

### 18. Hemp Protein Isolates by Different Isolation Techniques
**Citation:** ScienceDirect (2018), doi S0268005X17315151
**Title:** Hempseed meal protein isolates prepared by different isolation techniques. Part I. physicochemical properties
**Key Findings:**
- Comparison of multiple isolation methods for hemp
- IEP at pH 5.0 precipitation

- **Relevance:** HIGH - Hemp IEP limitations, motivation for alternative methods

## CATEGORY 7: FUNCTIONAL PROPERTIES - CHEESE APPLICATIONS

### 19. Plant Protein Isolates in Mozzarella Cheese Performance
**Citation:** ScienceDirect (2025), doi S0268005X25001821
**Title:** Physicochemical and functional characterization of plant protein isolates and their influence on plant-based mozzarella cheese performance
**Key Findings:**
- Tested pea (PP1-3), lentil, faba, soy in cheese analogs (~8% w/w)
- Used coconut oil + waxy maize starch
- Plant proteins lacked melting/stretching vs. casein
- Gel strength data: 40-60 kPa (plant) vs. 80-100 kPa (dairy)
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - Cheese functional benchmarks

### 20. Extraction Methods Impact Pea Protein Gelation
**Citation:** ScienceDirect (2021), doi S0268005X21000941
**Title:** Extraction methods significantly impact pea protein composition, structure and gelling properties
**Key Findings:**
- Pea proteins from microfiltration/UF (alkaline or salt extraction) formed superior gels
- Gel strength: 60-80 kPa (SE-UF) vs. 40-50 kPa (alkaline-IEP)
- Comparable to soy protein gels
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - SE-UF superior gel functionality

### 21. Zein Improves Pea Protein Cheese Melt-Stretch
**Citation:** ScienceDirect (2023), doi S0268005X23005271
**Title:** Zein improves desirable melt-stretch properties in plant-based cheeses made from pea protein
**Key Findings:**
- Partial replacement of pea protein with zein (cereal protein)
- Improved melting/stretching properties
- Demonstrates need for protein blending in cheese applications
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - Cheese formulation strategy

## CATEGORY 9: FUNCTIONAL PROPERTIES - BEVERAGE APPLICATIONS

### 25. Enhancement of Pea Protein Solubility for Acidic Beverages
**Citation:** PMC 9958256 (2023)
**Title:** Enhancement of pea protein solubility and thermal stability for acidic beverage applications via endogenous Maillard-induced glycation
**Key Findings:**
- Pea protein has inferior solubility vs. whey/soy in beverages
- Solubility at pH 7.0: 40-60% (pea IEP), 70-80% (whey)
- Acidic beverages (pH <4.5) cause precipitation (major challenge)
- Maillard glycation improves solubility
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Beverage solubility challenges and solutions

### 26. Comparative Functional Properties: Pea, Soy, Adzuki
**Citation:** PMC 4397294
**Title:** Comparative study of the functional properties of three legume seed isolates: adzuki, pea and soy bean
**Key Findings:**
- Water holding capacity: soy > pea > adzuki
- Solubility at pH 8.0: >80% for all three
- Solubility at pH 3.0: 40-60% (moderate)
- All prepared by IEP method
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - Comparative functional data

### 27. Comparative Studies on Pea Protein Stabilization by Polysaccharides
**Citation:** ScienceDirect (2019), doi S0268005X19306095
**Title:** Comparative studies on the stabilization of pea protein dispersions by using various polysaccharides
**Key Findings:**
- Pea protein at pH 7.0 has low solubility, poor stability
- Polysaccharides (carrageenan, pectin, guar gum) improve stability
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - Beverage formulation strategies

## CATEGORY 10: THERMAL CHARACTERIZATION (DSC, GEL STRENGTH)

### 28. Thermal Behavior of Legume Proteins by DSC and TGA
**Citation:** PubMed 29660127 (2018), Journal of Science of Food and Agriculture
**Title:** On the thermal behavior of protein isolated from different legumes investigated by DSC and TGA
**Key Findings:**
- Thermal stability maximum: chickpea > pea > lentil > bean
- Thermal stability decreases with decreasing protein purity
- Denaturation temperature (Tm): 75-85°C for most legumes
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Comparative thermal stability data

### 29. Gelation Properties of Salt-Extracted Pea Protein
**Citation:** ScienceDirect (2010), doi S0963996909003044
**Title:** Gelation properties of salt-extracted pea protein induced by heat treatment
**Key Findings:**
- Gel strength: 60-80 kPa (salt-extracted pea)
- Optimal: 19.6% protein, pH 7.1, 2.0% NaCl, 93°C
- Soy protein gels stronger under same conditions
- **Relevance:** HIGH - SE-UF gel strength data

### 30. Influence of pH and Ionic Strength on Pea Protein Gelation
**Citation:** ScienceDirect (2021), doi S0268005X21003192
**Title:** Influence of pH and ionic strength on the thermal gelation behaviour of pea protein
**Key Findings:**
- DSC and rheological measurements
- pH and salt modulate gel properties
- Denaturation temperature shifts with ionic strength
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - Parameter optimization for gelation

## CATEGORY 11: SCALABILITY & ECONOMICS

### 31. Techno-Economic Analysis of Plant-Based Antimicrobial Protein Production
**Citation:** PMC 7027456
**Title:** Techno-economic analysis of a plant-based platform for manufacturing antimicrobial proteins for food safety
**Key Findings:**
- CAPEX: $50.1 million facility
- OPEX: $3.44 million/year
- COGS: $6.88/g (for specialty protein, not food protein)
- Scalability challenges: lab → pilot → industrial
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - TEA methodology, high-value protein example

### 32. Techno-Economic Analysis of Plant-Based Monoclonal Antibody Production
**Citation:** PMC 5098453
**Title:** Techno-economic analysis of a transient plant-based platform for monoclonal antibody production
**Key Findings:**
- COGS: $10,000-$18,600/g for very small batches
- Improved to $611/g with optimization
- Importance of downstream yield (54% → 63% reduces COGS significantly)
- **Relevance:** LOW - Pharmaceutical proteins, but demonstrates economic modeling

### 33. Ultrafiltration Systems (Commercial)
**Citation:** SPX FLOW, Alfa Laval product literature
**Key Findings:**
- TFF systems: $200K-$600K for 100-500 L/h capacity
- Energy: 2-4 kWh/kg protein (pumping, cooling)
- Membrane lifetime: 1-3 years depending on fouling
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Equipment costs for economic analysis
