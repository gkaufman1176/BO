# Comparative Analysis of Plant Protein Extraction Methods
## Literature Review: Hemp, Sunflower, and Pumpkin Proteins

**Date:** 2025-11-08
**Prepared by:** Research Analysis

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This comprehensive literature review compares three protein extraction methods across three plant sources (hemp, sunflower, pumpkin), evaluating yield, functionality, and flavor/sensory properties. Key findings:

**CRITICAL LIMITATION:** Limited literature exists specifically comparing the proposed "Two-Step Salt Extraction (TSP → NaCl)" method. Most research focuses on:
- **Alkaline Extraction + Isoelectric Precipitation (AE-IEP)** - Industry standard
- **Salt Extraction + Ultrafiltration (SE-UF)** - Emerging alternative
- **Mild-acidic extraction + precipitation** - For polyphenol control

**KEY FINDINGS:**
1. **Yield**: Alkaline extraction achieves highest yields (60-87%) but at the cost of functionality
2. **Functionality**: Salt extraction preserves native structure and thermal stability better than alkaline methods
3. **Flavor**: Acidic/neutral pH extraction prevents polyphenol oxidation and browning
4. **Phytic Acid**: High alkaline pH (>10) effectively removes phytic acid; TSP chelation potential unconfirmed in literature

## 2. DETAILED FINDINGS BY METHOD

### 2.1 ALKALINE EXTRACTION + ISOELECTRIC PRECIPITATION (AE-IEP)

**Description:** Extract at pH 8-12 with NaOH, precipitate at pH 4-5 (isoelectric point)

#### ADVANTAGES:
✓ **Highest protein yields** (60-90% depending on source)
✓ **High protein purity** (>90%)
✓ **Effective phytic acid removal** (83-88% at pH >10)
✓ **Simple, low-cost process**
✓ **Industry standard** (well-established)

#### DISADVANTAGES:
✗ **Severe protein denaturation** at pH >11
✗ **Polyphenol oxidation** and browning
✗ **Poor functional properties** (reduced solubility, gel strength, emulsion stability)
✗ **Formation of lysinoalanine** (toxic)
✗ **High wastewater generation**
✗ **Color issues** (dark brown isolates)

#### KEY DATA BY PLANT SOURCE:

**HEMP:**
- Yield: 60.6-87.3% (Helstad 2022; Liu 2024)
- Protein content: 90.3% dry weight
- Phytic acid: 0.557-0.595 g/100g (83-88% reduction)
- Thermal stability: pH-HPI showed partial denaturation
- Functionality: Poor solubility (~9-20% at pH 7), inferior to SE-HPI

**SUNFLOWER:**
- Yield: ~90% at pH 10-12 (but only 30-50% at pH 8-9)
- Protein content: >94%
- Polyphenolic issues: Chlorogenic acid oxidizes, covalent binding to proteins
- Color: Dark, unacceptable
- Functionality: Poor despite high purity

**PUMPKIN:**
- Yield: 43-70% depending on conditions
- Protein content: 90-94%
- Optimal pH: 9-10 (milder than hemp/sunflower)
- Thermal stability: Tm >90°C
- Functionality: Better than hemp/sunflower AE-IEP

**CONFIDENCE: HIGH** - Well-documented method across all three sources

---

### 2.2 SALT EXTRACTION + ULTRAFILTRATION/PRECIPITATION (SE-UF)

**Description:** Extract at neutral pH (6-7) with 0.5-2M NaCl, concentrate via ultrafiltration or precipitation

#### ADVANTAGES:
✓ **Preserves native protein structure** (higher Tm)
✓ **Better functional properties** (solubility, emulsion, foam, gel)
✓ **Prevents polyphenol oxidation** (light color)
✓ **No toxic byproduct formation**
✓ **Better flavor profile**
✓ **Higher thermal stability**

#### DISADVANTAGES:
✗ **Lower yields** than alkaline extraction (for some sources)
✗ **Higher salt concentration required** (2M for best yields)
✗ **Requires membrane filtration** (higher equipment cost)
✗ **Limited phytic acid removal** at neutral pH
✗ **Residual salt** in final product (requires washing)

#### KEY DATA BY PLANT SOURCE:

**HEMP:**
- Yield: 81.6-87.3% with 0.5M NaCl (comparable to AE-IEP!)
- Protein content: 86.6-88.1%
- Thermal stability: SE-HPI showed higher Tm, less denaturation
- Structure: 46.53% albumin (vs 20.92% in AE-HPI)
- Functionality: Superior solubility, emulsion activity
- Color: Lighter than pH-HPI
- Phytic acid: Minimal removal at neutral pH

**SUNFLOWER:**
- Yield: 46-59% at pH 7.3/0.3M NaCl (lower than AE-IEP)
- Yield: 23-26% at 2M NaCl (improves with higher salt)
- Protein content: >94%
- Chlorogenic acid: <0.2% (excellent removal via adsorption at pH 6)
- Color: Light, acceptable
- Functionality: Excellent foaming and emulsifying
- Thermal stability: Tm 91-102°C (native structure preserved)

**PUMPKIN:**
- Yield: Data limited, varies with NaCl concentration
- Protein content: 90-94%
- Thermal stability: Tm >90°C
- Functionality: Good gel strength, emulsion, foam
- Process: Often combined with HPP (high-pressure processing) for enhancement

**CONFIDENCE: HIGH for hemp, MEDIUM for sunflower/pumpkin**

---

### 2.3 TWO-STEP SALT EXTRACTION (TSP → NaCl)

**Description:** Extract with trisodium phosphate (0.5M, pH 8-9), precipitate with NaCl (0.3M, pH 8-9)

#### CRITICAL RESEARCH GAP:

**NO DIRECT LITERATURE FOUND** specifically describing this extraction method for hemp, sunflower, or pumpkin proteins.

#### THEORETICAL ANALYSIS:

**Potential advantages:**
- TSP provides alkaline pH (11-12) for protein solubilization
- TSP has chelating properties that could bind phytic acid-metal complexes
- Constant pH avoids isoelectric precipitation (may preserve structure better)
- NaCl precipitation via salting-out at alkaline pH

**Concerns:**
1. **Polyphenol oxidation**: pH 8-9 is still alkaline enough to cause browning
2. **Incomplete precipitation**: NaCl at 0.3M is low for effective salting-out (literature suggests 2-3M needed)
3. **Phytic acid removal**: Effective at pH >10, but mechanism unclear (TSP chelation vs pH alone)
4. **Protein denaturation**: pH 8-9 may still denature some proteins
5. **No washing step**: Constant pH means no isoelectric precipitation washing benefit

**RELATED FINDINGS:**

- **Phosphate in extraction**: Phosphate buffers commonly used for protein extraction
- **TSP properties**: pH 11-12, chelating agent, used in meat processing
- **Phytic acid dissociation**: Occurs at pH >10-11
- **NaCl precipitation**: Effective at 2-3M (Hofmeister series: sulfate > phosphate > chloride)

**CONFIDENCE: LOW** - No experimental validation found; theoretical only

## 3. FUNCTIONALITY ANALYSIS

### 3.1 Thermal Stability (Native Structure Preservation)

**RANKING (Best to Worst):**

1. **Salt Extraction (SE-UF)** - BEST
   - Hemp SE-HPI: Tm ~82°C, native structure
   - Sunflower: Tm 91-102°C, highest thermal stability
   - Pumpkin: Tm >90°C
   - **Evidence**: DSC analysis shows SE proteins resist thermal degradation better

2. **Mild Alkaline + Precipitation** - GOOD
   - Pumpkin AE-IEP (pH 9): Tm >90°C (milder conditions preserve structure)
   - Hemp Helstad method (pH 10.5): Good but some denaturation

3. **Strong Alkaline + IEP** - POOR
   - Hemp pH-HPI (pH 12+): Partial denaturation, polymerization
   - Sunflower (pH 10-12): Severe denaturation at pH 12+
   - **Evidence**: Lower Tm, structural changes visible in DSC

**CONFIDENCE: HIGH** - Consistent across multiple studies

---

### 3.2 Gel-Forming Ability

**KEY FINDINGS:**

- **Extraction method is critical**: Native proteins gel better
- **Pumpkin**: Least gelation concentration 8% w/v (good gelation)
- **Hemp**: SE-HPI superior gel strength vs pH-HPI
- **Sunflower**: Heat-treated isolates show reduced gel strength
- **Mechanism**: Denaturation during extraction reduces gel-forming ability

**RANKING:**
1. Salt extraction (preserves native structure for gelation)
2. Mild alkaline (moderate gel properties)
3. Strong alkaline (poor gel properties due to denaturation)

**CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM** - Limited direct comparison data

---

### 3.3 Emulsion Stability

**KEY FINDINGS:**

**HEMP:**
- SE-HPI: Superior emulsion activity vs pH-HPI
- Both methods: Poor solubility limits emulsion performance

**SUNFLOWER:**
- SE-UF: Excellent emulsifying capacity (56.2 m²/g at 0.6M NaCl)
- AE-IEP: Fair emulsifying properties despite poor solubility
- Ultrafiltration > Isoelectric precipitation for emulsion capacity

**PUMPKIN:**
- High emulsification capacity after enzymatic hydrolysis
- HIUS treatment: Significant improvement in emulsification

**RANKING:**
1. Sunflower SE-UF (best emulsifier)
2. Pumpkin SE or modified proteins
3. Hemp SE-HPI (moderate)
4. All AE-IEP methods (limited by poor solubility)

**CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM** - Different measurement methods across studies

---

### 3.4 Foaming Properties

**KEY FINDINGS:**

**SUNFLOWER:**
- SE proteins: Excellent foaming properties
- AE-IEP: Fair foaming despite poor solubility
- Mechanism: Degree of denaturation affects foaming

**PUMPKIN:**
- Foam stability: 57.14%
- High foam capacity at neutral/alkaline pH
- Acetylation improves foaming capacity

**HEMP:**
- SE-HPI: Better foaming stability than pH-HPI
- 0.5M NaCl reduces foaming stability

**RANKING:**
1. Sunflower SE-UF (excellent)
2. Pumpkin SE (good)
3. Hemp SE-HPI (moderate)
4. All AE-IEP (limited by denaturation)

**CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM**

## 4. FLAVOR/SENSORY ANALYSIS

### 4.1 Polyphenolic Content

**SUNFLOWER** - Highest polyphenolic challenge:
- Main compound: Chlorogenic acid (1-4%)
- AE-IEP: Severe oxidation, covalent binding to proteins, dark color
- SE-UF (pH 6 + resin adsorption): <0.2% chlorogenic acid (excellent)
- Color: Light vs dark brown

**HEMP:**
- Polyphenols abundant in hulls
- AE-IEP: Browning occurs at high pH
- SE-HPI: Light color, minimal browning
- Dehulling: Very effective for reducing polyphenols

**PUMPKIN:**
- Lower polyphenolic content than sunflower
- Processing methods (roasting, fermentation): 80-100% removal

**RANKING (Best Polyphenol Removal):**
1. **Pumpkin** - Naturally lower, easy to remove
2. **Sunflower SE + adsorption** - <0.2% residual
3. **Hemp SE or dehulled** - Minimal browning
4. **All AE-IEP methods** - Severe browning issues

**CONFIDENCE: HIGH**

---

### 4.2 Phytic Acid Content

**REMOVAL EFFICIENCY BY METHOD:**

1. **AE-IEP (pH >10)** - 83-90% reduction
   - Hemp: 0.557-0.595 g/100g (from 22.5 mg/g in press cake)
   - Sunflower: 1.05 mg/g with optimized conditions
   - Mechanism: Phytic acid dissociates at pH >10-11

2. **SE-UF (neutral pH)** - Minimal reduction
   - Phytic acid remains complexed with proteins
   - Some removal via ultrafiltration/diafiltration

3. **Protein isolation + washing** - 80% reduction
   - Pumpkin: Effective with proper washing
   - Removal of phytic acid-metal complexes

4. **Thermal processing** - 31% reduction
   - Pumpkin: Roasting, fermentation effective
   - Not extraction-dependent

**RANKING (Best Phytic Acid Removal):**
1. **AE-IEP at pH 10-12** (83-90%)
2. **Protein washing + isolation** (80%)
3. **Combined thermal + extraction** (moderate)
4. **SE-UF alone** (minimal)

**TSP METHOD HYPOTHESIS:** May chelate phytic acid-metal complexes, but NO DATA FOUND

**CONFIDENCE: HIGH for AE-IEP, LOW for TSP method**

---

### 4.3 Bitter Taste and Astringency

**MECHANISM:**
- Polyphenols/tannins activate TAS2R bitter receptors
- Protein-tannin complexes cause astringency
- Chlorogenic acid (sunflower) particularly problematic

**SENSORY FINDINGS:**

**ALL PLANT PROTEINS:**
- Commercial isolates: Bitter (1.5-5.8/10), astringent, raw material flavor
- Off-flavors: Beany, grassy, hay-like (aldehydes, ketones)
- Sunflower: Additional bitterness from chlorogenic acid

**METHOD IMPACT:**

1. **SE-UF (pH 6-7)**:
   - Prevents polyphenol oxidation
   - Light color, better sensory
   - Lower bitterness

2. **AE-IEP**:
   - Polyphenol oxidation increases bitterness
   - Dark color, astringent
   - Poor sensory acceptability

3. **Dehulling/Pre-treatment**:
   - Most effective (hulls contain 80%+ tannins)
   - Significantly reduces bitterness

**COMPARATIVE RATINGS** (Literature sensory panels):
- Soy isolate: Best performer (benchmark)
- Pea: Moderate (sulfurous off-odor)
- Hemp/Sunflower/Pumpkin: Limited direct sensory data
  - Hemp: "Mild earthy, slightly nutty"
  - Sunflower: "Slightly sweet and nutty" (but bitter with polyphenols)
  - Pumpkin: "Unique fragrance"

**RANKING (Best Sensory):**
1. **SE-UF methods** (light color, lower bitterness)
2. **Dehulled + extraction** (best overall)
3. **AE-IEP** (dark, bitter, astringent)

**CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM** - Limited direct sensory comparisons

---

### 4.4 Color Quality

**COLOR MEASUREMENT:**

**SUNFLOWER:**
- AE-IEP: Dark brown (chlorogenic acid oxidation)
- SE-UF + adsorption: Light color, acceptable
- ΔE: Significant difference (SE >> AE-IEP)

**HEMP:**
- pH-HPI: Browning at high alkalinity
- SE-HPI: Lighter, more acceptable
- Dehulled: Light and bland

**PUMPKIN:**
- Generally lighter than sunflower
- Extraction method still impacts color

**RANKING (Best Color):**
1. **SE-UF + adsorption** (lightest)
2. **Mild-acidic extraction** (light)
3. **Dehulled + any method** (light)
4. **AE-IEP** (dark brown, unacceptable)

**CONFIDENCE: HIGH**

- SE-UF: Higher residual phytic acid

**POLYPHENOLS:**
- SE-UF: Can be very low with adsorption (<0.2%)
- AE-IEP: Oxidized and bound to proteins (unusable)

**CONFIDENCE: HIGH**

## 6. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT

### 6.1 Literature Quality

**STRENGTHS:**
✓ Recent high-quality studies (2022-2024)
✓ Peer-reviewed journals
✓ DSC thermal analysis data available
✓ Some direct method comparisons (esp. hemp)

**WEAKNESSES:**
✗ **Different labs, conditions** - not directly comparable
✗ **Limited head-to-head trials** across all three sources
✗ **Measurement method variability** (gel strength, emulsion, foam)
✗ **Cultivar differences** not controlled
✗ **Pre-treatment variability** (defatting, dehulling methods differ)

### 6.2 Data Gaps

**CRITICAL GAPS:**

1. **TSP → NaCl two-step method**: NO LITERATURE FOUND
   - Theoretical only
   - Mechanism unclear
   - No yield, functionality, or sensory data

2. **Direct three-way comparison**: Limited
   - Most studies examine ONE method on ONE source
   - Few studies directly compare AE-IEP vs SE-UF on same material

3. **Sensory evaluation**: Sparse
   - Limited organoleptic panels
   - No standardized bitterness/astringency scales
   - Color measured, but sensory acceptance not

4. **Thermal stability**: Good for some, missing for others
   - Hemp: Good data
   - Sunflower: Some data
   - Pumpkin: Limited DSC data

5. **Scale-up data**: Limited
   - Most studies lab-scale
   - Helstad 2022 includes pilot-scale (valuable!)

### 6.3 Contradictions

**IDENTIFIED CONTRADICTIONS:**

1. **Sunflower yields**:
   - Some studies: 90% at pH 12
   - Others: 30-50% typical
   - Resolution: pH 12 needed for 90%, but causes denaturation

2. **Salt concentration effects**:
   - Higher NaCl should improve extraction (salting-in)
   - But 2M NaCl gives lower yields than 0.5M
   - Resolution: Salting-out occurs at very high concentrations

3. **Functionality vs purity**:
   - AE-IEP: High purity (>94%) but poor functionality
   - SE-UF: Lower purity (86%) but better functionality
   - Resolution: Denaturation affects function more than purity

**NO MAJOR UNRESOLVED CONTRADICTIONS**

### 6.4 Assumptions and Caveats

**KEY ASSUMPTIONS:**

1. **Defatted meal as starting material** - most studies use this
2. **Lab-scale extrapolates to industrial** - may not hold
3. **Cultivar/variety effects minimal** - likely false (sunflower especially)
4. **Functional property tests comparable** - different methods used
5. **Polyphenol content stable** - varies by harvest, storage

**CAVEATS:**

⚠ **Plant source variability**: Sunflower shows highest variability in polyphenolics
⚠ **Process conditions matter**: Small pH changes drastically affect outcomes
⚠ **Equipment requirements**: SE-UF requires ultrafiltration (cost)
⚠ **Water usage not quantified**: Sustainability assessment incomplete
⚠ **Energy consumption not compared**: Critical for industrial decisions

### 7.3 Plant Source-Specific Recommendations

#### **HEMP (Cannabis sativa):**

**BEST METHOD:** Salt Extraction + Ultrafiltration (SE-UF)
- **Why?** Achieves 81-87% yield (comparable to AE-IEP)
- Preserves native structure and functionality
- Light color, better sensory
- **Trade-off:** Residual phytic acid (can be washed)

**ALTERNATIVE:** Alkaline extraction (pH 10.5) + IEP
- **Why?** If phytic acid removal is critical
- Helstad 2022: 60.6% yield, 90.3% purity, good phytic acid removal
- **Trade-off:** Some denaturation

**AVOID:** Strong alkaline (pH 12+)
- Severe denaturation and browning

**APPLICATION-SPECIFIC:**
- **Cheese/egg analogs** (need gelation): SE-UF strongly preferred
- **Beverage** (need solubility): SE-UF strongly preferred
- **Nutrition bar** (functionality less critical): AE-IEP acceptable if cheaper

**CONFIDENCE: HIGH**

---

#### **SUNFLOWER (Helianthus annuus):**

**BEST METHOD:** Salt Extraction (pH 6) + Polyphenol Adsorption + UF
- **Why?** Prevents chlorogenic acid oxidation (critical!)
- Light color (<0.2% chlorogenic acid)
- Excellent functional properties
- **Trade-off:** Lower yield (46-59%)

**ALTERNATIVE:** Mild-acidic extraction + resin adsorption
- **Why?** Best color and sensory
- Protein content >94%
- **Trade-off:** Very low yield (23-26%)

**AVOID:** Alkaline extraction (pH >8)
- Catastrophic browning
- Chlorogenic acid covalently binds proteins
- Dark, bitter, astringent, unacceptable

**APPLICATION-SPECIFIC:**
- **ANY application requiring white/light color**: MUST use neutral/acidic pH
- **Cheese analogs**: SE-UF with adsorption
- **Beverages**: SE-UF with adsorption (color critical)
- **Egg analogs**: SE-UF (functionality critical)

**CONFIDENCE: VERY HIGH** - Polyphenol issue well-documented

---

#### **PUMPKIN (Cucurbita pepo):**

**BEST METHOD:** Mild Alkaline (pH 9-10) + IEP
- **Why?** Good yield (43-70%)
- Good functionality (Tm >90°C)
- Lower polyphenolic content than sunflower
- **Trade-off:** Moderate on all metrics

**ALTERNATIVE:** HPP + Ultrasound-assisted extraction (HPP+UAE)
- **Why?** Enhanced functionality
- 62% yield, 76% purity
- Improved solubility, emulsion, foam
- **Trade-off:** Equipment cost, scalability uncertain

**ALTERNATIVE:** Salt Extraction
- **Why?** If functionality is critical
- Limited data but promising

**APPLICATION-SPECIFIC:**

### 7.5 Assessment of TSP → NaCl Two-Step Method

#### **EVIDENCE ASSESSMENT: NO LITERATURE SUPPORT**

**Theoretical evaluation:**

**Potential advantages (UNPROVEN):**
- TSP creates pH 11-12 (good for protein extraction and phytic acid dissociation)
- TSP may chelate phytic acid-metal complexes (mechanism uncertain)
- Constant pH may preserve structure better than pH-shift (speculative)
- NaCl precipitation avoids isoelectric point (benefit unclear)

**Concerns (LIKELY):**
- pH 8-9 still alkaline enough for polyphenol oxidation (sunflower especially)
- NaCl 0.3M too low for effective salting-out (literature suggests 2-3M needed)
- Constant alkaline pH may still denature proteins
- No washing step via isoelectric precipitation (misses purification benefit)
- TSP chelation of phytic acid: theoretically possible but unproven

**Predicted performance vs established methods:**

**THE FUNDAMENTAL TRADE-OFF:**

```
HIGH YIELD + PHYTIC ACID REMOVAL  ⟷  FUNCTIONALITY + FLAVOR
        (Alkaline extraction)            (Salt extraction)
```

**You cannot maximize both with current methods.**

## 8. CONCLUSION

### Key Takeaways:

1. **Salt Extraction (SE-UF)** is the **CLEAR WINNER** for **functionality and flavor**
   - Preserves native protein structure (higher Tm)
   - Prevents polyphenol oxidation (especially critical for sunflower)
   - Excellent functional properties (emulsion, foam, gel, solubility)
   - Light color, better sensory

2. **Alkaline Extraction (AE-IEP)** is the **WINNER** for **yield and phytic acid removal**
   - Highest protein recovery (60-90%)
   - 83-90% phytic acid reduction at pH >10
   - BUT: Severe denaturation, browning, poor functionality, bad flavor

3. **Plant source matters:**
   - **Sunflower**: MUST avoid alkaline pH (chlorogenic acid disaster)
   - **Hemp**: SE-UF achieves comparable yields to AE-IEP (breakthrough!)
   - **Pumpkin**: More forgiving, multiple methods work

4. **TSP → NaCl two-step method: NO LITERATURE SUPPORT**
   - Cannot be recommended without experimental validation
   - Theoretical concerns about effectiveness
   - High risk of poor performance

5. **Application drives method choice:**
   - **Functional foods** (cheese, egg, beverages): SE-UF mandatory
   - **Nutritional supplements** (powder, bars): AE-IEP acceptable
   - **Premium products**: SE-UF or dehulling + SE-UF

### Final Recommendation:

**For developing plant-based cheese, egg, or beverage products:**

→ **Use Salt Extraction + Ultrafiltration (SE-UF) at neutral pH (6-7)**
→ **Pre-treat with dehulling if possible** (removes polyphenols/tannins)
→ **Post-treat with phytase if phytic acid removal critical**
→ **For sunflower: Add polyphenol adsorption step (mandatory)**

**Evidence quality: HIGH** for this recommendation
